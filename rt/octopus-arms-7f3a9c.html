How octopus arms think for themselves

An octopus has about 500 million neurons, and roughly two thirds of them sit in its arms rather than its brain. Each arm can sense, grasp and react on its own, which is why a severed arm can still reach and pull at food for a while.

Researchers think this lets the animal explore many things at once: the brain sets a goal, such as "go there", and the arm works out how.
